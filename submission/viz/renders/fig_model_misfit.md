(a) Among the games in which the model gives the underdog P(win) < alpha, the number of underdog wins observed divided by the number the model expects (sum of its own probabilities), pooled over time controls, by rating band. At alpha = 0.10 the main model gives 1.05 / 0.91 / 0.77 for <2200 / 2200-2499 / 2500+ and the interaction model 1.04 / 0.97 / 0.78: the extra rating x gap terms repair the 2200-2499 tail but not the 2500+ tail, where underdogs still win about 22% less often than predicted; at alpha = 0.05 the pooled ratio is 0.87 (main) vs 0.99 (interaction), and the 2500+ values at alpha = 0.05 rest on only 360 games (main). (b) Change of each critical gap (interaction - main, 18 cells per alpha): alpha 0.20 -5 to +15, 0.10 -40 to +15 (mean -6; 16 of 18 inside the main interval), 0.05 -120 to -10 (mean -54; only 4 of 18 inside). So the alpha = 0.10 results are robust to the model form, but the main model's alpha = 0.05 critical gaps are probably too large and should be quoted with this caveat. Limitation: the intervals in (a) ignore tournament clustering and parameter uncertainty (too narrow); the cause of the 2500+ misfit is untested (the 2500+ band mixes 2500 and 2700+ players; elite events have many short draws).
