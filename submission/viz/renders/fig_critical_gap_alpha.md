Critical gap (smallest gap at which the model's P(underdog wins) falls below alpha) for alpha from 0.01 to 0.30, at each band's reference average rating, with the published 95% intervals at the four reported alphas. On the 0.005-step grid from 0.05 to 0.30 the ordering classical < rapid < blitz holds at 295 of 306 points; the 11 exceptions are Black-underdog ties at gap 0 for alpha of 0.265 or more (even an equal-rated Black player has P(win) below alpha there, so classical and rapid both give 0). At alpha = 0.10, 3 of 18 critical gaps lie beyond the observed gaps of their slice (rapid, white, 2500+: 385 vs data to 300; blitz, white, 2500+: 450 vs data to 350; blitz, black, 2500+: 380 vs data to 350). Limitation: 'inside the data' only means that every 50-point bin up to that gap holds at least 100 games, so the tail still rests on a few hundred games per bin; beyond a gap of 900 the model is flat by construction, so the smallest alphas have no critical gap at all. Two model caveats: strong underdogs (2500+) win only 0.77 times the expected number in the model's tail at alpha = 0.10 (interaction model: 0.78; pooled over time controls, mostly classical games), so the 2500+ critical gaps, at least in classical, are probably too large; and at alpha = 0.05 the interaction model gives critical gaps 54 points smaller on average (4 of 18 inside the main interval), so the main model's alpha = 0.05 values should only be quoted with that caveat (fig_model_misfit).
