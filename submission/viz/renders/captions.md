## fig_gap_distribution

Rating gap |Elo(white) - Elo(black)| for the 862,599 analysis games that have an underdog, in 25-point bins (bar height = % of that time control's games; the last bar pools gaps of 900+). Median gap: classical 152, rapid 188, blitz 174 points; 8.3% of all games have a gap of 400+ and 1.7% of 600+ (classical 6.7%, rapid 12.9%, blitz 11.4% at 400+). Limitation: the large-gap tail, where the most surprising upsets live, is too thin to see on this linear scale (the sparsest 25-point bin below 600 still has 577 games); the gaps are between published FIDE ratings, which lag a player's current strength, especially for fast-improving juniors.

## fig_outcomes_by_gap

Observed outcome rates of the lower-rated player per 50-point gap bin (the last bin pools 600+), all 862,599 games with an underdog, pooled over the underdog's colour and the rating band; the grey strip counts the games per bin (its scale differs per panel: classical has 5.7x the games of blitz). Underdog win rate in the 0-49 bin vs the 400-449 bin: classical 25.9% vs 5.8%, rapid 33.0% vs 9.1%, blitz 37.0% vs 11.4%; the classical draw rate is 42.2% in the 0-49 bin. Limitation: pooling mixes rating levels and colours (see the Simpson figure); every bin has at least 100 games, and no confidence intervals are shown (n per bin is in the strip).

## fig_heatmap

Each cell is the observed share of games won by the lower-rated player (draws count as not winning), by 50-point gap bin and 100-point bin of the average rating (both colours pooled); the lower row counts the games per cell. The green step marks, per rating row, the first gap bin whose win rate is below 10%: in classical it sits at a median gap of 350 points for average ratings 1600-1999 but 200 for 2200-2599. Limitation: cells with fewer than 100 games are hatched (the coloured cells hold 99.4% of the games shown); the step is the first crossing of a noisy observed rate, not a fitted boundary, and draws, which rise with rating, are ignored.

## fig_simpson

Observed classical results of the lower-rated player against the rating gap (50-point bins), pooled (left) and split (middle, right; the light grey line repeats the pooled curve). At gaps of 50-199 the underdog scores 0.407 points per game with White but 0.328 with Black (win rate 21.9% vs 16.4%); between near-equal players (gap of 25 or less) the draw rate rises with the rating band from 33% (<2200) to 49% (2200-2499) and 63% (2500+), so at the same gap a strong underdog wins less often (11.5% vs 22.2% at 50-199 for 2500+ vs <2200) but draws far more. Limitation: the band is defined by the average rating, so bands also differ in their gap mix and in who plays (juniors, open events); colour and band are shown one at a time, not jointly, and bins with fewer than 100 games are not drawn.

## fig_upset_points

Every upset at the 10% level (11,114 games: 9,007 classical, 1,483 rapid, 624 blitz) plotted at its rating gap and average rating; an upset is defined by the empirical model: the underdog won although the model gave it P(win) < 10%, or drew although P(not losing) < 10% (604 of the upsets are such draws). The 20 rarest (smallest model probability, q from 0.0136 to 0.0205) are highlighted and listed; all of them are classical, with gaps of 626-1218 points. Limitation: this shows where upsets occur, not how likely they are (dense regions are dense mainly because many games are played there), and the empty band along the left edge is built into the definition (where the model gives the underdog 10% or more, no result counts as an upset); a very large gap often means an outdated or wrong rating rather than a sensational game; full links are in rarest_upsets.csv.

## fig_set_compare

Larger cleaned set (1,129,532 games, interim/clean_games) vs the analysis set (863,971). Most of the 265,561 dropped games lack a FIDE rating for a player (238,168); that share peaks in 2023 (41% of the year's games). 2.7% of the cleaned set has an unknown time control; 23,938 of these games still had both ratings and were removed by that filter. Games with at least one FIDE-titled player rise from 45% to 54%, because only 17% of the dropped games have one: the analysis set over-represents titled, rated players. Limitation: 'titled' counts FIDE titles (GM, IM, FM, CM and the women's titles) recorded in the PGN, and the rating and game-length mixes are not shown.

## fig_model_vs_data

Observed underdog win rate (markers, Wilson 95% intervals) per 50-point gap bin against the model's like-for-like prediction (line: mean of each game's own predicted P(win), using its own gap and average rating), by rating band and time control. In the 229 bins with at least 100 games the prediction lies inside the observed interval in 193 (84%; about 95% would be expected if the model were exact), and the n-weighted mean error per panel and colour ranges from -2.0 to +0.6 percentage points (largest: classical, 2500+, white underdog, -2.0 pp). Limitation: the prediction is in-sample (the model was fitted to these games; the held-out check is in the calibration figure), the intervals ignore the clustering of games in tournaments, and evaluating the curve at one reference rating instead would shift the prediction by at most 0.27 pp in any well-filled bin of the 2200-2499 band (up to 4.9 pp in the wide <2200 band). The clearest misfit is classical 2500+, where underdogs win less often than predicted (model inside the interval in only 5 of 14 bins); the interaction model (rating x gap terms) does not remove it (5 of 14 bins, -2.2 pp; see fig_model_misfit).

## fig_verified_split

Win rate of the White underdog, average rating 2200-2499: the model curve at the band's reference rating with its 95% band (as drawn in the notebook) against observed win rates of all games and of the two subsets split by whether both ratings are confirmed by the FIDE list within 100 points (76% of all games are verified). ## fig_critical_gap_alpha

Critical gap (smallest gap at which the model's P(underdog wins) falls below alpha) for alpha from 0.01 to 0.30, at each band's reference average rating, with the published 95% intervals at the four reported alphas. On the 0.005-step grid from 0.05 to 0.30 the ordering classical < rapid < blitz holds at 295 of 306 points; the 11 exceptions are Black-underdog ties at gap 0 for alpha of 0.265 or more (even an equal-rated Black player has P(win) below alpha there, so classical and rapid both give 0). At alpha = 0.10, 3 of 18 critical gaps lie beyond the observed gaps of their slice (rapid, white, 2500+: 385 vs data to 300; blitz, white, 2500+: 450 vs data to 350; blitz, black, 2500+: 380 vs data to 350). Limitation: 'inside the data' only means that every 50-point bin up to that gap holds at least 100 games, so the tail still rests on a few hundred games per bin; beyond a gap of 900 the model is flat by construction, so the smallest alphas have no critical gap at all. Two model caveats: strong underdogs (2500+) win only 0.77 times the expected number in the model's tail at alpha = 0.10 (interaction model: 0.78; pooled over time controls, mostly classical games), so the 2500+ critical gaps, at least in classical, are probably too large; and at alpha = 0.05 the interaction model gives critical gaps 54 points smaller on average (4 of 18 inside the main interval), so the main model's alpha = 0.05 values should only be quoted with that caveat (fig_model_misfit).

## fig_upset_rule

Critical gap at alpha = 0.10 computed from the model for every average rating (line), with the three published band values (dots with 95% intervals) and the number of games by average rating (strips). For a White underdog in classical the critical gap falls from 375 points at an average rating of 1800 to 235 at 2600 (blitz: 510 to 450). The line is solid only where the rating row holds at least 500 games and the critical gap lies within the gaps observed at that rating (grey area). Limitation: the rating effect is a single linear term in the model, so the smooth decline is an assumption, and the data say it is too simple: among games the model gives P(win) < 0.10, underdogs win 1.05 times the expected number at average ratings below 2200 but 0.91 times at 2200-2499 and 0.77 times at 2500+, so the line is probably too high for strong players. A model with rating x gap terms (interaction model) brings 2200-2499 to 0.97 but leaves 2500+ at 0.78, so this strong-player misfit remains a limitation (fig_model_misfit); the open-ended 600+ bin of the heat table caps the grey area at 650.

## fig_calibration_check

Held-out check: the model was refitted on half of the tournaments and evaluated on the other half (426,692 games in 48 bins of time control x underdog colour x 100-point gap, the last pooling 700+). Mean absolute error 0.39 percentage points n-weighted and 0.72 unweighted; the largest bin error is -2.2 pp (blitz, black underdog, gap 600-699, n = 373). In rapid and blitz at gaps 200-599 the observed rate is below the prediction in 15 of 16 bins (mean -1.1 pp), so on new tournaments the fast-chess curves are, if anything, slightly too generous to the underdog. Limitation: one random split of tournaments; bins pool all rating levels, which hides the strong-player misfit (fig_model_misfit); every bin has at least 100 games, so none is greyed.

## fig_model_misfit

(a) Among the games in which the model gives the underdog P(win) < alpha, the number of underdog wins observed divided by the number the model expects (sum of its own probabilities), pooled over time controls, by rating band. At alpha = 0.10 the main model gives 1.05 / 0.91 / 0.77 for <2200 / 2200-2499 / 2500+ and the interaction model 1.04 / 0.97 / 0.78: the extra rating x gap terms repair the 2200-2499 tail but not the 2500+ tail, where underdogs still win about 22% less often than predicted; at alpha = 0.05 the pooled ratio is 0.87 (main) vs 0.99 (interaction), and the 2500+ values at alpha = 0.05 rest on only 360 games (main). (b) Change of each critical gap (interaction - main, 18 cells per alpha): alpha 0.20 -5 to +15, 0.10 -40 to +15 (mean -6; 16 of 18 inside the main interval), 0.05 -120 to -10 (mean -54; only 4 of 18 inside). So the alpha = 0.10 results are robust to the model form, but the main model's alpha = 0.05 critical gaps are probably too large and should be quoted with this caveat. Limitation: the intervals in (a) ignore tournament clustering and parameter uncertainty (too narrow); the cause of the 2500+ misfit is untested (the 2500+ band mixes 2500 and 2700+ players; elite events have many short draws).
